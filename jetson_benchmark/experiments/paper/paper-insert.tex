\paragraph{Isolated mask arithmetic.} In three sustained blocks per implementation, one addition of 128 synthetic coordinates averaged 36.981 $\mu$s and 185.579 $\mu$J of module input for the Linux Python helper, and 0.143 $\mu$s and 0.692 $\mu$J for the compiled TA loop. These amortized measurements exclude per-probe command, authentication and transport costs. The two implementations use different languages.

\paragraph{Information disclosed by reconstruction (replacement for Lemma 2).}
Fix an honest verification on encoded reference $x$ and probe $y$. A drone
observing its own output shares and the wireless device's reply learns
$u=\langle x,y\rangle$ and $v=\langle x,x\rangle$ in the protocol ring.
By correctness, summing each corresponding pair of shares yields $u$ and $v$
with probability one. For $y=(1,0,\ldots,0)$, the references
$(2,1,0,\ldots,0)$ and $(1,2,0,\ldots,0)$ both have $v=5$ and both pass
either experimental threshold, yet yield $u=2$ and $u=1$ respectively.
Their drone views are therefore distinguishable even given the same decision
and reference norm. A passive-drone privacy statement for the original
protocol must condition on the opened scalars as well as the drone's input.
In the TEE-masking variant Linux still reconstructs these scalars. In the
TEE-reconstruction variant the TA opens them and returns the decision bit.
In both variants Linux supplies the live vector and can derive its one-use
mask from the outgoing masked vector and the known fixed-point input.
